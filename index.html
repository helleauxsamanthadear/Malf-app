    The rules

    • One new thing at a time, a few days apart — so you know what to blame if skin gets weird.
    • Niacinamide only at night (it can pill under SPF).
    • Azelaic: start every other night, pea-size, whole face.
    • Later add Differin 2 nights/wk on non-azelaic nights — never both same night.
    • Painful/cystic breakouts → a dermatologist can prescribe. Worth it.
    🌊Curl routineEmbrace the wave — fewer products, better technique▶

    Waves need the opposite of the curly-girl internet: lightweight products, not much, and technique over slather. Only on wash days (2–3×/wk) — not daily.

    The whole kit (don't buy more)

    • Verb Ghost shampoo + conditioner (their pick for fine, frizzy, wavy — light enough to let the wave show)
    • One lightweight leave-in
    • One light styler — Verb Ghost Air Dry Whip keeps it all one brand, or Briogeo Curl Charisma gel
    • Skip creams & the Curl duo — too heavy, they flatten waves
    • Backup: Verb Hydrate for dry ends / winter, especially until the cut removes damaged length

    Wash day

    1
    Shampoo scalp, conditioner mid-to-ends, finger-comb through in the shower.
    2
    Get out — don't wring or towel-rough. Hair stays soaking.
    3
    Scrunch in leave-in, then gel — flip head, scrunch upward.
    4
    Hands off while drying. Dyson diffuser on low heat, low speed — cup sections up, don't press flat. Stop ~80% dry.
    5
    Once dry, scrunch out the crunch — break the gel cast into soft waves. Optional drop of oil.
    6
    Night: loose pineapple (top-of-head scrunchie) + satin pillowcase.

    Start now, refine after the cut. Build the habit cheaply now — but don't stockpile products. The cut removes damaged ends and adds curl-friendly layers, so your waves will behave differently (better) after.

          For the hard moments. Read whichever one you need.

          🩶When you're being hard on yourselfRead this. It's true.▶
          "I slacked off."
          Life got full and the optional stuff fell away — exactly what optional stuff does. Not failure.
          "I'm not consistent."
          You kept the lunch walk going the whole time — social, scheduled, protected. That's the hardest one, and you held it.
          "My journal sounds whiny."
          It reads negative because of when you reach for it. It's a venting valve, not a highlight reel.
          "Rereading this is cringe."
          It reads overblown because you're not in it anymore. That gap is proof you moved through it.
          "I have to fix everything."
          Pick one anchor — the smallest version that survives your worst day. Then add the next.
          📓How you journal nowDump hard, end on altitude▶
          1
          Dump first. Messy, unfair, repetitive. No editing. The paper can take it.
          2
          Then one line up. One sentence that isn't the wound. (Save it in the Today tab ↑)
          3
          Done. Two lines or two pages, both count.

          Guardrails

          • Keep it on paper, not a screen — especially before bed.
          • Don't reread old entries for a good while. Rereading too soon is where the spiral lives.
          • "I showed up and wrote this" is the altitude line on a hard day.
          🌙Rough-night toolkitFor when sleep won't come▶
          • Run a few rounds of the reset breath (Today tab).
          • Get morning light tomorrow — resets a rattled clock.
          • Don't chase it with a long nap the next day.
          • One bad night isn't a pattern. A wired night after travel has a cause.
          One honest note. If the erratic moods start showing up without a trigger, or scare you a little, that's worth a real conversation with a doctor — not because anything's wrong with you, but because moods sometimes have fixable causes, and you deserve to feel steady, not just cope well.
